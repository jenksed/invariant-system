# Mine Writing Fragments

Use when the user has ideas, anecdotes, claims, examples, phrases, evidence, or arguments but is not ready to force them into an outline.

Run a focused interview that extracts **fragments**: self-contained pieces of raw material that may later become paragraphs, scenes, examples, transitions, or arguments.

Rules:

- preserve heterogeneous material rather than prematurely normalizing it;
- ask for concrete examples and experiences, not only themes;
- separate claims from supporting evidence;
- capture memorable language when it is the user's own wording;
- append fragments to a durable raw-material document;
- do not turn collection into drafting until enough material exists or the user explicitly changes phases.

Completion means the raw-material pool is rich enough that shaping can choose among real substance rather than inventing filler.
